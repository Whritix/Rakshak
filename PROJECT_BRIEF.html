⚠️ The Problem in Plain English

During naval patrols or border operations, commanders don't suffer from a lack of data—they are drowning in it:

  • Gigantic Images vs. Tiny Targets: A military satellite raster can be 20,000 × 20,000 pixels. A ship or vehicle is only 15 pixels across. Normal AI squishes the whole image, making small targets disappear.
  • Dark Vessels & Piracy: Hostile warships, smugglers, and surveillance trawlers turn off their AIS transponders in key chokepoints (like the Strait of Malacca), hiding from coastal radar.
  • Cloud Failure in Combat: Standard AI (ChatGPT, cloud vision APIs) requires an internet connection. In combat under electronic warfare jamming and radio silence (EMCON), cloud tools fail 100%.
  • Siloed Feeds: Naval satellite maps and Army drone cameras stay isolated with no unified fusion.

💡 Winning Answers to Tough Judge Questions

Judge asks: "Why SQLite instead of a production database like PostgreSQL or MongoDB?" "In a commercial cloud app, Postgres or MongoDB makes sense. But Rakshak is built for air-gapped combat environments—inside a warship's combat ops room or a mountain bunker. Running a separate database daemon introduces memory overhead, port conflicts, and failure points if the service crashes. SQLite is an in-process engine in a single file (rakshak.db). With Write-Ahead Logging (WAL), reads and writes happen concurrently with sub-millisecond speed, and commanders can physically copy the mission database onto an encrypted USB drive in 2 seconds."
Judge asks: "How do you detect tiny 15-pixel boats in a huge 20,000x20,000 satellite image?" "Standard AI downsamples the whole image to 640×640, erasing small boats. Rakshak implements a Sub-Pixel Window Slicing Engine: we slide an overlapping 1024×1024 window across the full-resolution GeoTIFF with a 20% stride. Each tile retains 100% of its native resolution. We then translate the detections back to global GPS coordinates and apply global Non-Maximum Suppression to remove boundary duplicates."
Judge asks: "How does the threat scoring actually work?" "Our Threat Engine uses a composite multi-factor scoring model (0–100):
• Restricted Zone Breach (+30): Distance check against military exclusion zones (e.g. Mumbai ODA, Karwar Naval Base).
• Tactical Clustering / Convoy (+20): Our fast spatial grid indexes targets moving within 500 meters of each other.
• Dark Vessel / AIS Anomaly (+50): When radar detects a metallic reflection with no matching AIS transponder signal."
